## 1. What the system does (one paragraph)

We parse scientific-paper PDFs (ACL Anthology, arXiv, etc.) into structured text, extract
a knowledge graph of (subject, predicate, object) triples with an LLM (Qwen3-14B)
constrained by an ontology, and evaluate the resulting KG by an **unsupervised
citation-prediction** task: train a knowledge-graph embedding (KGE) on KG + paper–concept
links *with citation edges held out*, then test whether a paper's learned embedding ranks
its true citation neighbours above other papers. We compare **two ontologies** (CEO and
scinex), **three KGE models** (TransE, ComplEx, RotatE), and **two training objectives**
(margin-ranking vs. self-adversarial).

## 3. Evaluation protocol

- **Task:** for each query paper, rank all candidate papers by embedding similarity;
  ground truth = its real citation neighbours (held out of training).
- **Metrics:** Hits@1/5/10 and MRR (mean reciprocal rank), reported as **mean ± std over
  5 random seeds**.
- **Two scorings, always reported together:**
  - **bidirectional** — predict *any* citation neighbour.
  - **temporal-filtered (`filt`)** — rank only candidates published ≤ the query's year
    (a paper can't cite the future); the more correct "reference-prediction" setup, on a
    smaller test set.
- **Honest model selection (added 2026-06-24):** the 132 eval papers are split into a
  fixed **validation half (66)** and **test half (66)** (`--split-seed 42`, independent of
  training seed). All tunable hyperparameters (γ in particular) are chosen on validation;
  final numbers are reported on the **untouched test half**.
- **Random baseline ≈ 0.2% Hits@1** over ~500 candidate papers → results should be read as
  **lift over random**, not against 1.0 (1.0 is not the target for unsupervised citation
  prediction).

## 6. Key findings (claims the data supports)

1. **Self-adversarial negative sampling is the decisive lever for RotatE** — +0.19–0.21
   MRR over margin (test split), lifting the best result from ~0.43 to ~0.60. This is the
   model's native training objective (Sun et al. 2019); plain margin loss under-trains it.
2. **The effect is model-specific.** Self-adversarial helps RotatE; it *hurt* ComplEx and
   TransE in tuning (their best remains margin). So the reported pipeline pairs RotatE with
   self-adversarial and leaves the others on margin.
3. **The two ontologies are statistically equivalent** on citation prediction (CEO filt
   0.594 vs. scinex filt 0.606 — within one std), at near-equal triple counts. The refined
   scinex ontology **matches CEO's downstream quality without degrading it**; it does not
   *beat* it on this metric.
4. **The temporal-filtered ("reference-prediction") task scores ~equal-or-higher** than
   bidirectional and is the more correct citation setup.
5. **All results are well above the ~0.2% random baseline** — a strong proof-of-signal that
   KG content learned purely from paper text predicts real citation links.

## 7. Honest caveats (state these in the paper)

- **Test-split numbers are on 66 papers** (half the eval pool) — the cost of an honest
  held-out validation split. The all-papers numbers (§5) cover 132 but had no separate
  tuning set.
- **Ontology result is a tie, not a win.** If the paper's contribution is the ontology
  comparison, citation prediction alone is too blunt to distinguish them; a per-triple
  precision or ontology-coverage analysis would be needed to say more.
  → **STILL A TIE on the second axis too (2026-08-27, n=240) — see "Per-triple extraction
  quality" below.** Per-triple judging on 18 papers gives corpus-weighted strict precision
  **CEO 34.7% vs scinex 39.6%**, bootstrap difference −4.9 pts with 95% CI [−22.5, +13.9].
  An earlier n=80 reading ("CEO beats scinex, 46.2 vs 34.4") **did not replicate and is
  withdrawn**. CEO does keep a lenient-precision edge (66.8% vs 59.9%). ⚠ Extractor was
  `qwen3:8b` on a laptop, NOT Qwen3-14B — not comparable to the KGE numbers above, and not
  the paper's figure. The KGE/citation-prediction tie is a different axis and is unaffected.
- **Report lift-over-random**, not absolute MRR vs. 1.0.
- **Self-adversarial hurts ComplEx/TransE** here, partly a mismatch with the cosine-similarity
  prediction path — don't over-generalise "self-adversarial is better."

Currently 155 papers. **Recommendation: not required for the current claims.**
- More papers *lower* absolute MRR (bigger candidate pool = harder ranking) — they add

- Worth doing **only** if a reviewer would call 155 too small for the generalisation claim.

### Four fixable failure modes (`hands_off.md` §24.3)

1. **Domain/range violations** — the largest error class; `employs`/`affiliatedWith`/`locatedIn`/
   `publishedIn` used as generic verbs. Fix: post-parse type check per predicate.
2. **Direction inversions** — `splitFrom`, `supports`, `evaluates`, `configures`. Fix: extend the
   existing direction guards.
3. **⛔ Schema placeholder leak (bug)** — `(Kalman filter, mentions, AcademicPaper)`: the model copies
   an ontology *class name* out of the prompt as a value. Fix: reject objects matching a class name.
4. **Author-contribution sections mined** — CRediT statements produce `affiliatedWith` triples. Fix:
   skip `Author contributions` / `Declarations` / `Acknowledgements` at extraction time.

**Sample size caveat:** n=240 with 4–6 samples per predicate cell. Enough to rank the predicate
*bands* and to show the ontology difference is not resolvable; **not** enough to quote a per-predicate
percentage. The corpus-weighted figures carry a ±13-point 95% interval.

| | CORRECT | PARTIAL | INCORRECT |

**The disagreement is almost entirely at the PARTIAL boundary.** The two judges agree on what is

argument for reporting **both** a strict and a lenient number, as this project already does, rather
than a single precision figure.

**This makes the Table 8 gap more robust, not less.** Gemini is the *harsher* judge — it scores qwen
at 13.6% where Claude scored it 24.5% — and Claude's extraction still earns 89.1% from it.

### ✅ FIXED 2026-08-28 — a second pipeline bug: `evaluates` could never be correct

`kg_builder._is_valid_entity()` requires every node to contain `[a-zA-Z]{2,}`, so a bare numeric
object is deleted **after** the guards pass. But the prompt defines `evaluates` as
`EvaluationMetric → ExperimentalResult` and its own GOOD example is `(<Metric>, evaluates, 88.5)`.
So the pipeline **asks for a value it then throws away**: `(precision, evaluates, 0.923)`,
`(RMSE, evaluates, 14.76)` and `(F1-score, evaluates, 9.23)` were all produced and all silently
dropped. Only the malformed, wordy `evaluates` triples survive into `triples.json` — which is why
`evaluates` scores **0% strict** in Table 4. The same filter deletes short acronym objects
(`< 3 chars`): `(PNext, comprises, SF/SD/SP)` vanished the same way.
**FIXED (Session 26).** `_is_valid_entity(text, predicate, role)` now takes the predicate and the
role, and admits a measurement value (`_VALUE_RE`: `88.5`, `0.923`, `14.76 ms`, `97%`, `1.2e-4`) as the
**object** of a value predicate (`evaluates`, `achieves`, `reports`) only — a bare number is still
rejected everywhere else, so the filter has not been loosened generally. All-caps acronyms (`SF`,
`SD`, `F1`) are exempted from the `len < 3` floor. Verified on 11 cases, then measured by re-ingesting
Claude's stored replies: **+11 triples, 268 → 279**, `evaluates` 1 → 6. Table 7 carries the detail.
qwen3:8b's column still predates the fix (no stored replies to re-ingest).

### ⛔ Guard bug found and fixed while running Table 7 (`kg_extraction/fixed_extractor.py`)

`_subject_in_sentence()` wrapped the subject in `\b…\b`. `\b` only marks a word/non-word transition,
so **any subject ending in a non-word character never matched** — `Segment Anything Model (SAM2)` is
dropped even when it appears verbatim in the source sentence. Since "Full Name (ABBR)" is the standard
way a paper introduces a model, this silently deleted nearly every parenthetical subject:
**2 of 2,132 triples** in the qwen3-8b corpus have a subject ending in `)`. Fixed with `_contains_term()`
(lookarounds applied only on the alphanumeric side). **All corpus numbers above were produced with the
buggy guard** — the fix can only add recall, and its effect is unmeasured until re-extraction.

### ⭐ Finding 1 — the models differ on TYPING, not on concept identification

C1 spans 63.7–88.8% (1.4×). **C4 spans 38.8–82.5% (2.1×).** The concepts extractors pull out are
mostly real and present in the sentence; what separates a good extractor from a bad one is whether
it respects the predicate's domain→range. Under the old single CORRECT/PARTIAL/INCORRECT rubric
these were one number and this split was invisible.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Finding 2 — VOLUME DOES NOT BUY COMPLETENESS. C2/C5 are flat.

ministral-14b produces **4.3× more triples** than qwen3-235b and fires on **4.2× more paragraphs**
(69.1% vs 16.4%). Its completeness scores: **C2 31.2% vs 31.2% — identical**; C5 34.4% vs 28.1%,
**+6.3 points**. Every model sits in a 25–34% band on both criteria.

This is the strongest result of the new framework and it was unobtainable before it. **Extracting
four times as much does not capture four times as much of the paper** — the extra volume is spent
re-stating what was already captured, enumerating pairwise combinations, and mining boilerplate,
not on the paragraphs that were being missed. Both the sparse and the dense extractor leave roughly
two-thirds of each paper's important content unrecorded, and they leave *different* thirds.

### ⭐⭐ Finding 3 — C6 falls monotonically with volume; rank correlation is perfect

| extractor | triples | C6 | verdicts (n=6 papers) |

More triples means more chances for two of them to conflict, and the conflicts are concentrated in
predictable places: **superlatives with the scope qualifier stripped** ("best AUC" asserted of three
different methods), **metric nodes bound to two values** because the class label was dropped, and
**surface-variant splitting** that lets incompatible claims attach to what should be one node.

The single clearest contradiction in the study: gemma4-31b asserts both `(VGG16) achieves (F1 w-avg
(0.23))` and `(VGG16) achieves (over 90% accuracy)`. The clearest structural violation: ministral-14b's
`(route prediction model) --comprises--> (route prediction model)`, a node containing itself.

### ⚠ Caveats

- **n=40 triples / 16 paragraphs / 6 papers per model.** The C1/C3/C4 bands are reasonably sized;

  total, perfect rank correlation with volume), not as a per-model point estimate.
- **Single rater, by design.** claude-opus-5 in-session labelled every item. **This is a stated
  limitation of the C1–C6 track, not an unfinished step** (decided 2026-09-07): there is no second
  labeller and therefore no κ for C1–C6, and none is planned. The old-rubric κ figures (Table 9)

  applying a written rubric — criteria like C4's domain/range test involve genuine judgement calls
  (e.g. `(OC-SORT) publishedIn (CVPR 2023)` was scored PARTIAL on the grounds that a Model stands in
  for the required AcademicPaper; CORRECT and INCORRECT are both defensible readings), and their
  reproducibility across raters is unmeasured.
- **CEO only.** The scinex side of these four extractors has not been run.
- claude-opus-5 is also an extractor elsewhere in this project. It did **not** judge its own
  extraction here — the four extractors judged are all non-Claude.
