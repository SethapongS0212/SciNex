It was reported that 2 factors, which were the mechanical and chemical factors, were the causes of spallation. For the mechanical factors, the spallation can occur by 2 main causes which were the CTE mismatch between the oxide and substrate and the poor surface adhesion of oxide scale . For CTE mismatch, it seemed to be the primary cause of spallation36. However, it was dependent on the phase structure of the substrate and developed oxide scale37, so it can be said that the spallation was actually due to the chemical compositions and operating conditions resulting in the atomic/ion diffusions specifying the phase formed. For the surface adhesion, it was also due to the chemical compositions36,38–40 microstructures, defect structures41, oxidation atmospheres42–44, oxidation temperature40 and period of oxidation45,46 which selectively promoted the solid-state diffusion of ions affecting the oxide -substrate adhesion47. Therefore, it can be summarized that the mechanical factors were actually the intermediate effect affecting the spallation. The real primary effect was chemical factors which were in good agreement with the work of Hardell et al.48 demonstrated that the changes in mechanical properties was a result of microstructural and morphological changes, thermal conditions and surface chemistry. Also, Takeda et al.49 reported that the spallation of the scale was influenced by the elemental compositions.

Section: Data analytics

Text:
In addition, several methods, such as Artificial Neural Network (ANN), K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN), and Random Forest (RF), had their own critical parameters, which were the numbers of hidden layers and nodes, the numbers of nearest neighbors, and the numbers of estimators, respectively. Results from the latter machine learning methods are typically reported using different critical hyperparameters. Each experiment underwent testing through K-Fold Cross-Validation, where K is set to be 3, as depicted in Fig. The dataset was divided into 3 chunks, and the training and testing processes were repeated for 3 rounds. In each round, 2 chunks were utilized for training to create a model, while the remaining chunk was employed to test the model. The training set can contain both original and oversampled data, whereas the test set comprised only the original samples; thus, it was ensured that the performance of each classification technique was reported from the original data. Due to the imbalanced ratio of spallation and non-spallation samples in the test set, the F1 score was chosen as the evaluation metric for the model, rather than accuracy. In the final step, the F1 scores from the 3 rounds were averaged to report the performance of each experiment.
