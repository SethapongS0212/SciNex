Section: 3.2 Discussion

Text:
By analyzing BERT and ROUGE-L scores, it is evident that both self-attention matrices (dashed line) and feed-forward network matrices (dotted line) follow the same trend as full matrix corruption (solid black line). However, the feed- forward matrices exhibit a stronger effect, with their impact being comparable to corrupting all matrices simultaneously. A closer examination reveals that the degradation rates of Attn-Q, Attn-K, and Attn-V matrices are similar, which aligns with their equivalent roles within the Transformer architecture, as depicted in Fig. In contrast, within the feed-forward network, the FF-Down matrix appears to have the most severe impact. This is likely because it is positioned at the very end of each layer, right before the final output, making its corruption the most disruptive. However, this study is subject to several limitations. These in- clude testing only on a relatively small model due to computational resource constraints, focusing on a single model, and applying random corruption by setting parameters to zero. Future research should explore a broader range of models, particularly those uti- lizing the Mixture of Experts (MoE) architecture [10] to better understand how different model structures respond to parameter corruption. Additionally, alternative corruption methods should be considered, such as replacing values with random numbers or using the maximum or minimum representable values instead of simply setting them to zero.

Section: 4 Summary

Text:
This study examines the effects of parameter corruption on a Large Language Model (LLM) by methodically altering various compo- nents and assessing the resultant discrepancies from the baseline outputs. The trials demonstrate that heightened corruption results in a more significant decline in model performance, as assessed by BERT and ROUGE scores. Testing the Llama -3.2-1B-Instruct was conducted on questions from the GLUE-QNLI dataset to measure deviations from the baseline model. While all layers appear to be equally important at lower corruption levels, higher corruption lev- els suggest that later layers may be more critical. Further analysis of self-attention and feed-forward matrices shows that feed-forward components, particularly the Down matrices, have a stronger influ- ence on model degradation. The study acknowledges limitations such as testing on a single, relatively small model and using a uni- form zeroing-out approach for corruption. Future research should extend to larger and more diverse architectures, including Mix- ture of Experts (MoE) models, and explore alternative corruption techniques. These insights could inform future efforts to hard-wire LLM parameters into microchips, enabling better fault detection and designing redundancy strategies for critical components like the Down matrices.
