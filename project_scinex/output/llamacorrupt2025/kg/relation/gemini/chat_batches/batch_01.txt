Section: Abstract

Text:
This study explores the effects of parameter corruption in a large language model (LLM) by altering its weights and evaluating perfor- mance. Experiments involve corrupting different layers and matrix types, including Self-Attention and Feed-Forward components, with performance assessed using BERT and ROUGE scores. Testing the Llama -3.2-1B-Instruct model was performed on the GLUE-QNLI dataset. Results show that increased corruption leads to greater degradation, with Feed-Forward matrices having a stronger impact especially in the Down matrices. According to the study, later layers are more important for performance than those that come before them. These results shed light on possible future chip implemen- tations of LLM, which may help guide the design of fault-tolerant systems by taking vulnerable parameter placement into account. We assess Meta's Llama -3.2-1B-Instruct [4] a 16-layer Transformer model acquired from Hugging Face [5] The model underwent evaluation on the GLUE-QNLI dataset [6] with perfor- mance assessed through BERT's F1 score and ROUGE metrics [7] Our experiments indicate that randomly zeroing 10–15% of the model's parameters signifies the threshold at which the LLM starts deteriorating. Moreover, the experiments show that corruption in the earlier layers has less impact than in the later layers, suggesting that the sensitivity of the model increases towards the deeper layers. In addition, we have noted that the feed-forward components are more susceptible to parameter corruption than the self-attention matrices. The above data suggests that the submodules of the Trans- former architecture respond to perturbations in a wide range of ways. Our research offers insights into the structural vulnerabilities of LLMs and their resilience to internal disruptions, especially regard- ing future LLM implementations on microchips [8] By highlighting these limitations, we seek to promote the advancement of language models that are more robust and fault-tolerant, thereby ensuring their dependability in crucial applications, particularly those imple- mented on hardware systems .

Section: 2.2 Selected Test Data

Text:
We selected the Question-answering Natural Language Inference (QNLI) dataset from the General Language Understanding Evalua- tion (GLUE) benchmark for this study [6] The GLUE-QNLI dataset contains over hundred thousand question-answer pairs, but in prac- tice, we randomly sampled thousand questions for testing. In this experiment, we do not use the provided answers from the dataset, as our goal is not to evaluate the knowledge of the LLM. Instead, we use the questions as prompts, serving as input to the LLM. The model's responses, before any parameter corruption is introduced, are then used as baseline answers for comparison. These baseline answers may differ from the original dataset answers but serve as a consistent reference for evaluating the impact of parameter corruption.

This test will be evaluated based on Bidirectional Encoder Represen- tations from Transformers (BERT) and Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation (ROUGE) scores [7] BERT will assess the quality of the language understanding and prediction, while ROUGE will measure the overlap between the generated content and the reference content.
