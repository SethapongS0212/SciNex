This design enabled automatic correction for variations in image misalignments and brightness fluctuations without human intervention, thus reducing biased selection and result manipulation. The image processing methodology consisted of six steps. Initially, each pixel of the images was converted to a lightness (L) value. Subse quently, three processing steps were undertaken: detection, correction of rotation and inclination, and normalization of light variations in the reference spectral band. Finally, the correction values from the reference band were projected onto the analysed band. This projection reduced uncertainty in the experimental setup and enhanced images of varying qualities into high-quality data suitable for machine learning analysis. Instead of scanning and selecting the highest absorbed wavelengths, as in other spectrophotometer methods, F-score analysis was employed for feature selection to choose the four most impactful wavelengths (features) for model training and validation. During prediction, cross- validation was also used to mitigate the risk of overfitting. Four machine learning algorithms, SVP, RF, MLP, and XGB, were utilised.
