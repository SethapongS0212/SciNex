Section: 3.7.1. Machine learning application

Text:
The application of machine learning (ML) to predict concentration has shown satisfactory performance. The ML methodology, incorpo rating feature selection and cross-validation, accelerates calculation and effectively mitigates overfitting. In the case involving concentrations ranging from 12 to 16 concentrations, a maximum of five minutes is required for image processing and prediction. When applied to chemical compounds with a distinct colour change, such as KMnO4, the method produced a low error of 1.48 %, which is superior to other low-cost spectrophotometers with reported errors ranging from 3 % to 12 % [14,38]. The method also demonstrated satisfactory performance in predicting Carbosulfan concentrations, with an error of approximately 3 %. However, at lower concentrations of Carbosulfan, the method failed to detect changes in lightness below 500 mg⋅L−1, where the average lightness value change was less than 0.5 L* per 50 mg⋅L−1. The relationship between changes in lightness value (absorbance) and chemical concentrations was observed not to strictly adhere to the Beer-Lambert law, which is typically applied by UV–Visible spectro photometers, as the most effective algorithms exhibited non-linear behaviour. This behaviour has been previously observed and reported by Randhawa et al. [47] and was captured in this study through the use of the whole wavelength for analysis, in contrast to conventional UV–Visible spectroscopy which utilizes only a single wavelength.
