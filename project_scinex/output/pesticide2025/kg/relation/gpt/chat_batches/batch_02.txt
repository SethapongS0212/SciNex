Section: 2.4.4. Model selection with Cross-Validation

Text:
Cross-validation is a technique employed to estimate the perfor mance of a model on unseen data [37]. This technique involves parti tioning the available data into multiple segments, training the model on a subset of these segments, and subsequently testing the model on the remaining segment, as illustrated in Fig. This procedure is reiterated with varying subsets, and the outcomes are averaged to yield a robust measure of the model's performance. The goal is to prevent overfitting, where a model performs well on training data but poorly on new data. To ensure a fair comparison of model performance in this study, K was selected to be 5. The Random Forest (RF) [32], Support Vector Regression (SVR) [33], Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) [34], and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGB) [35] algorithms, which are readily accessible packages, were utilized to train models with 80 % of the images . These algorithms were selected based on their performance during the development stage, considering that light absorbance behaviours do not strictly follow the linear relationship of the Beer-Lambert law, and the data exhibited some noise.
