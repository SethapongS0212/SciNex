Strabismus , defined as ocular misalignment during fixation, is a common ophthalmic disorder affecting approximately 1–3.9% of the population, including both children and adults1,2. Depending on the direction of deviation, strabismus is classified into four types: esotropia (inward), exotropia (outward), hypertropia (upward), and hypotropia (downward). Persistent strabismus can lead to amblyopia (commonly known as "lazy eye"), consequently impairing binocular vision and depth perception. Beyond visual consequences, both children and adults with visible strabismus often experience psychosocial challenges, including stigmatization, anxiety, and depression3,4. Recognition of the psychosocial effects of strabismus underscores the importance of counseling for patients and their families. Early diagnosis and treatment of strabismus are essential to lessen these adverse outcomes and prevent long-term visual impairment. However, diagnosis of strabismus typically requires direct examination by a pediatric ophthalmologist and the use of specialized tests, such as the Krimsky test, cover–uncover test, or alternate prism cover test, which require significant patient cooperation. This dependence presents a substantial barrier to effective screening in the general population, especially in rural or underserved regions where ophthalmologic services are limited5.

Section: Object detections

Text:
RT-DETR is a transformer -based object detector designed for real-time performance. It consists of a backbone, a hybrid encoder, and a transformer decoder with auxiliary prediction heads. The model efficiently processes multi- scale features by decoupling intra- scale interaction and cross- scale fusion. Its Intersection over Union (IoU)–aware query selection improves the quality of initial object queries, while the decoder iteratively refines them to generate bounding boxes and confidence scores. RT-DETR has demonstrated strong performance in both speed and accuracy . For example, RT-DETR-L achieves 53.0% mean average precision (mAP) on COCO val2017 and 114 frames per second (FPS) on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU, while RT-DETR-X achieves 54.8% AP, outperforming existing YOLO-based detectors18. The training objective combines three loss functions: Focal loss, Generalized IoU (GIoU) loss, and L1 loss. Focal loss addresses class imbalance by down-weighting easy negatives, while GIoU loss measures overlap and spatial alignment between predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes. L1 loss is used for bounding box regression. The relative contributions of these losses are controlled by weighting coefficients during training, enabling robust localization across varying object sizes. In addition, IoU-aware query selection initializes decoder queries using encoder features from regions with high predicted IoU scores.

SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) is an oversampling method used to address class imbalance. It was introduced by Chawla et al. This method operates by selecting instances that are proximate in the feature space, establishing a connection between these instances, and generating a new sample along this connection. Initially, a random instance from the minority class is selected, followed by the identification of its k nearest neighbors (usually k = 5). From these neighbors, another instance is randomly chosen, and a synthetic sample is generated at a randomly selected position between the original instance and its neighbor in the feature space.
