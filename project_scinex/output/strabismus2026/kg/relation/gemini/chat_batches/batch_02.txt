Participants who met the exclusion criteria were excluded, resulting in a final cohort of 150 participants (54 exotropia , 42 esotropia and 54 orthotropia). This resulted in 450 raw facial images (three per participant), 900 eye crops (two per image ), and a total of 8,100 landmark instances. For eye landmark detection , a deep learning model was evaluated using 4-fold cross-validation. Although nine landmark types were defined, 18 classes were implemented to distinguish between left and right eyes. As the dataset size was not evenly divisible by four, each fold contained 37–38 participants. In the step of feature engineering, as performed in Fig. 2 (D), five geometric ratios (RT1–RT5) were computed using selected landmarks in the horizontal line. According to the clinical expertise of the ophthalmologists in our research team, the pupil and corneal light reflex are approximately centered in orthotropic eyes during primary gaze, with relatively balanced visible scleral exposure on the nasal and temporal sides. Horizontal strabismus changes this relationship by shifting the corneal light reflex and altering the relative scleral show across gaze positions. On that basis, the selected landmarks were intended to capture three complementary geometric domains: overall eye width for normalization, nasal and temporal exposed scleral segments, and the horizontal position of the corneal light reflex relative to the limbal boundaries. We therefore used horizontal distances between selected landmarks as key features, specifically the medial canthus (P1), temporal canthus (P4), medial limbus (P7), temporal limbus (P8), and central corneal light reflex (P9). Based on prior ophthalmic knowledge within the research team, orthotropic eyes were expected to show relatively balanced nasal-versus- temporal relationships in primary gaze and predictable directional changes during lateral gaze. Accordingly, RT1 and RT4 were designed to reflect nasal and temporal scleral exposure, RT2 and RT3 to reflect the position of the corneal light reflex within the horizontal corneal span, and RT5 to reflect visible iris width as an internal reference during gaze change. In the primary position, RT1–RT4 were expected to remain relatively symmetric, particularly across the RT1:RT4 and RT2:RT3 pairs. During lateral gaze, directional shifts in these parameters were expected according to the side of fixation, with reciprocal changes between nasal and temporal measurements in the two eyes.
