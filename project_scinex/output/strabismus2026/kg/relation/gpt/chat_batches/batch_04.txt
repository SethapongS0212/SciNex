Our technical approach consists of two main components: Eye Landmark Detection using RT-DETR and Strabismus Classification using Random Forest. Additionally, the oversampling technique using SMOTE is utilized to aid in the training process. Eye landmark detection using RT-DETR We chose to use eye landmark detection using the deep learning model of RT-DETR due to the following key factors : The RT-DETR utilizes a mix of loss functions to enhance several facets of the detection process for assessing ocular alignment in strabismus , which depends on a precise detection of points surrounding the eye, the pupil, and the light reflex. The Focal loss is crucial for reducing class imbalance, ensuring the model does not neglect small, significant landmark points within the picture. The GIoU loss provides correct localization by removing poor bounding box overlap and spatial misalignment, which is essential for accurately framing minor features such as the corneal reflex. Finally, the L1 loss facilitates precise coordinate refinement with steady gradients, Scientific Reports | (2026) 16:18278 12 | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-48893-6 enhancing the predicted positions for optimal spatial accuracy . Overall, these losses allow the RT-DETR to be able to identify tiny eye landmarks in the picture of patients. The IoU-aware query selection mechanism in RT-DETR has an advantage in eye landmark identification, which requires precise localization of small areas.

Section: Oversampling technique using SMOTE

Text:
In this study, SMOTE was employed to address class imbalance in a dataset of limited size, which is crucial for improving model training stability and representation of minority classes. However, in some cases where many outliers are found, the synthetic data generated from noise may impact the overall results of the predictive model. To mitigate the issue of noise amplification, we conducted analyses of the synthetic samples by comparing the results against Over-sampling using SMOTE and cleaning using Edited Nearest Neighbors (SMOTE-ENN)23, which is a variant of SMOTE reducing synthesis noise and borderline data. Our prediction approach with the replacing SMOTE with SMOT-EEN resulted in an accuracy of 0.95, which is close to the accuracy of the original experiment using SMOTE. Therefore, the results suggest that the data synthesis was not negatively impacted by noise presented in the processed data. In addition, the dataset was first divided into training and testing sets prior to oversampling, ensuring that SMOTE was applied to the training data and thus avoiding any data leakage into the test set. This design maintains the validity of performance evaluation. Thus, the application of SMOTE under these controlled conditions provides an advantage in enhancing model generalization in small or imbalanced datasets while minimizing the risk of overfitting.

Section: Limitations and critical evaluation

Text:
Despite promising results, several limitations must be acknowledged. First , the dataset was collected from a single center and may not reflect real-world variability in age, ethnicity, lighting, or device quality. Second, landmark annotation was performed manually; inter-rater reliability was not formally assessed. One ophthalmologist provided the strabismus labels, and landmark annotation was performed by a researcher blinded to the clinical diagnoses. Third, although we used SMOTE and cross-validation to address imbalance, additional external datasets are needed to confirm generalizability. Moreover, we recognize that ratio-based classification— while interpretable—may have limitations in more complex forms of strabismus involving torsion or vertical components. Future work could explore deep feature fusion between landmark geometry and convolutional texture information to address mixed-type presentations. Future directions We propose several research extensions: 1. Dataset Expansion: Use a variety of smartphones, light settings, camera angles, and demographics (ages, ethnicities, and skin tones) in the training data to ensure the performance of the model can work well in a wide range of real-world scenarios and populations. Automated Annotation: Create and utilize bigger datasets more quickly without the need for expert labeling by using self-supervised or weakly-supervised learning.
