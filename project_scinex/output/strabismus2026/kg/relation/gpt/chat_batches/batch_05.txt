Section: Conclusion

Text:
In conclusion, using Real-Time Detection Transformer (RT-DETR) for high-precision eye landmark detection across three gaze positions—straight, left, and right—this study created an automated framework for binary screening of horizontal strabismus versus orthotropia. The geometric eye landmark ratios (RT1–5), which are the proportional distances between key ocular landmarks, such as the medial canthus, medial limbus, central corneal light reflex, and temporal canthus, were the basis of our proposed feature set. A Random Forest model, which showed high performance with an accuracy of 0.95, sensitivity of 0.96, and specificity of 0.94, was then used to classify these features. In addition, internal analysis suggested that several learned feature patterns exhibited alignment with established ophthalmic principles, especially with regard to the off-center displacement of the central corneal light reflex in strabismus patients and the visible sclera area during various gazes. However, these findings should be interpreted as model-level associations rather than direct causal explanations. Although an exploratory three-class analysis was also performed, further validation is required before reliable subtype classification can be claimed. Future research should focus on external validation of the proposed framework under more diverse and realistic image acquisition conditions, including low-light settings, variable facial orientations, and different smartphone cameras.
